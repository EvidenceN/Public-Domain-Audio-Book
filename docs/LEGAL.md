# Before a book is recorded: the public-domain checklist

Not legal advice: a working checklist, written on 2026-10-09 from general knowledge and to be checked with a lawyer
before the channel earns money. The site enforces the first point in code (a book without a public-domain record is
never shown); the rest is on whoever chooses the books.

1. **Public domain where we publish from: the United States.** In 2026 that covers works first published in 1930 or
   earlier; the line moves forward one year every 1 January. Write the reason into the book's file (`public_domain.basis`)
   and the date it was checked.
2. **Other countries count differently**, usually from the author's death (often 70 years). A book that is free in the
   US can still be protected elsewhere, and the site and YouTube are seen everywhere. Prefer authors who died more than
   70 years ago; note the others. The site says on every page that the rule is the United States'.
3. **Check the edition, not just the title.** A translation is its own work: an 1880 novel in a 1995 translation is not
   free. So are a modern introduction, footnotes, annotations, illustrations and a modernised text. Record only the
   public-domain text, and say in the file what was left out (`public_domain.excluded`).
4. **The source's own terms.** A site that transcribes public-domain books may put conditions on its files and its
   name. Project Gutenberg's name is a registered trademark: a copy that is sold or carries advertising must either
   follow its trademark terms or have its header, licence text and name removed. We read the text only, strip that
   wrapper, link to the source as "Source text", and do not use the name to promote the recordings. The About page says
   we are not connected with or endorsed by any source.
5. **YouTube and podcast platforms.** YouTube's monetisation rules turn away "reused" or "repetitious" content; a bare
   synthetic reading of a public text can be judged so. What helps: chapters, our own description and visuals, a spoken
   introduction of our own. A realistic synthetic voice must be disclosed where the platform asks; the site says so on
   every page.
6. **Keep the record.** For every book: the source address, the edition, the reason it is public domain, the date
   checked. That is the book's file, kept in git.
7. **If someone objects**, take the book down first (set its `published` date in the future) and look into it after.
